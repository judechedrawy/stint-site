Stint — Privacy Policy

Draft for the TestFlight beta. Last updated 3 October 2026.

This is a draft. I wrote this to match exactly what the app does, but it's not reviewed by a lawyer. NOTHING HERE IS LEGAL ADVICE.

This is written to be read by the people who use Stint, who are mostly 13 to 17. If any of it is unclear, email stint.beta@gmail.com and I'll fix the wording.

What we keep, and why

Your account. When you sign in, Firebase (Google's service, which we use to run Stint) keeps an account id and the email address you signed in with. That's how your stints come back when you get a new phone. With Sign in with Apple you can choose Hide My Email, and then all we ever get is a private relay address Apple made up for Stint. We never ask for your real one, and the app doesn't show the relay address anywhere. Apple also offers your name the first time; Stint doesn't use it. The name people see is the one you type.

Your profile. The name and emoji you chose, your weekly goal in hours, and whether you've finished setting up. You typed the name so you can change it whenever you like.

Your settings. Whether you share your streak, whether you share that you're in a stint, and your notification choices: the daily nudge and its time, "stint done", the four notifications from your circles (someone passes you, someone wants to join, someone new joins, the Monday recap) and your quiet hours. These are choices, not records: nothing stores when you were nudged or what you did about it.

Your stints — the timing only. When a stint started, how long it was meant to last, when it ended, and whether you finished it. That is all. Not what you were doing, not which apps were paused, not where you were.

Whether you're in a stint right now. While a stint runs, your account keeps when it's due to end, so Stint never sends you a notification in the middle of one. This is kept even if you don't share your stints, and nobody in your circles can see it. When the stint ends, it's cleared.

Your phone's notification address. If you allow notifications, each phone you use Stint on keeps an address that Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging made up for that copy of the app, and the phone's time zone, so your quiet hours fall at your night. That's all: no phone model, no number. It's deleted when you sign out on that phone or turn notifications off for Stint.

What the server keeps to send them. To send the notifications from your circles without overdoing it, Stint's server keeps a few short-lived notes: when you last got a "someone passed you" (so it's never more than three a day), a record that each notification went out (which doesn't name you, and is deleted after 14 days), and any "wants to join" held back until your quiet hours or your stint are over. On Monday it keeps a copy of each circle's points for last week so the recap can be sent; that's deleted after 14 days too. Nothing records whether you opened a notification or what you did next.

Your place in each circle. For every circle you're in, the circle keeps your name, your emoji, when you joined, and your points for this week and last. If you share your streak, that too. If you share being in a stint, when the current one ends.

Reports. If you report someone or a circle, we keep what you chose, anything you wrote, who you reported, and that it was you. Nobody in the circle is told who reported.

What never leaves your phone

  • Which apps you pick to pause. Stint needs Screen Time access to work: you connect it, then pick the apps that pause while a stint runs. Apple gives Stint only codes for those apps that it can't read, so Stint can't tell which apps they are. The codes stay on your phone, are left out of backups, and are never sent anywhere.
  • How much you use your phone, or which apps you open. Stint doesn't measure it and has no way to see it.
  • Your schedules, your appearance setting, and your history as your phone has it.

What we never collect at all

  • Your age or birthday. You told us you were 13 or older, once. We didn't keep the answer.
  • Where you are. A circle stores a time zone, chosen when the circle was made, so the week ends at the right midnight. If you allow notifications, your phone's time zone is kept too, for quiet hours. Nothing else about location, ever.
  • Contacts, photos, microphone, or anything you haven't offered.

Who can see what

WhoSees
The people in a circle with youYour name, your emoji, your points this week, when you joined. Your streak and "in a stint" only if you left those on.
People not in your circlesNothing. There is no way to search for you, and no public profile.
UsWhat's listed above, if we look at the database. We look when something breaks or somebody reports something.
Anyone elseNobody. We don't sell, share or trade any of it.

How long we keep it

As long as you have an account. Delete your account in the app and your profile, your settings, your phones' notification addresses, your stint backup and your place in every circle are deleted. What happens to a circle you started: it passes to whoever has been in it longest, and if you were the only one in it, the circle goes too.

If you signed in with Apple, deleting your account also tells Apple that Stint no longer has your permission. Stint disappears from the list of apps using Sign in with Apple in your iPhone's Settings, and your relay address stops working.

The phone forgets you too: your stint history, streak, schedules, settings and the apps you chose to block are wiped from it, and it starts fresh.

One thing stays in a circle: if you let somebody in, the circle still says they were "added by" the name you had. That's the circle's record of how they got there, and nothing else about you goes with it.

Reports are kept after that, because a report is about somebody else's safety as well as yours. Every report, whoever sent it, is deleted a year after it was sent.

What you can do

  • Download everything. You → Privacy & data → Download my data. It's a readable file.
  • Delete everything. You → Privacy & data → Delete account. It can't be undone.
  • Change what your circle sees. You → What your circle sees.
  • Turn Screen Time access off whenever you like, in your iPhone's Settings. Stint stops pausing apps straight away. A stint that's running ends early and doesn't count, and Stint asks you to connect again before the next one.
  • Leave a circle whenever you like, from the Circle tab.
  • Ask us anything, at stint.beta@gmail.com.

Where it's stored

Google Firebase, in a datacentre in Canada (Montréal region), and the code that sends notifications runs there too. Google acts for us and doesn't use your Stint data for its own purposes. A notification travels through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple's push service, which see the words on it (a first name and a circle's name) in order to deliver it.

To keep fake copies of the app out, your phone proves to Firebase that it's running the real Stint, using Apple's App Attest. That proof is about the app, not about you: it carries no name, account or phone number.

If things change

If we start collecting something new, this page changes before the app does, and the app will tell you. During the beta, expect small changes.

Grown-ups

If you're a parent or guardian and want to know what we hold about your teenager, or want it deleted, email stint.beta@gmail.com. Before Stint opens to the public there will be a proper parental-consent process; during this closed beta the testers are a small group who know us.
